01 / Why BlackFlag

Own the library

Streaming made music easy. It also made the library somebody else’s problem.

BlackFlag is for the other kind of setup: FLACs, MP3s, folders, weird old rips, carefully tagged albums, duplicate copies, missing artwork, and years of accumulated history on disk.

The point is not to replace the collection. The point is to make owning one less annoying.

02 / Inside

The useful stuff

One app around the files you already trust.

01

Play the local library

BlackFlag started as a desktop player. Browse the collection and play from the files on your machine instead of handing the library off to a service.

02

Scan without rewriting

Point BlackFlag at real music folders. It inventories the collection, tracks missing files, and leaves source audio alone during scans.

03

Review duplicates before cleanup

Exact and likely matches stay reviewable. Choose the keeper, save the decision, verify the plan, then move eligible files to quarantine instead of permanently deleting them.

Duplicates
04

Fix metadata with a safety net

Preview supported tag changes first. Tested MP3 and FLAC writes are backed up and verified before BlackFlag touches the source.

05

Find cover art when you ask

MusicBrainz and Cover Art Archive lookups are explicit, user-initiated actions. Artwork is cached under app data; it is not silently embedded into the music files.

03 / Rules

No library roulette

BlackFlag does not freestyle on your collection.

The dangerous parts stay deliberately boring. Show the plan. Verify it. Back up what can be backed up. Keep destructive actions narrow and reversible.

01

No permanent duplicate delete

Eligible cleanup moves files to app-data quarantine with restore support.

02

No automatic provider traffic

Online metadata and artwork lookup runs only after explicit user action.

03

No surprise metadata writes

Supported writes start with a dry run, verification, confirmation, and backup.

04

No broad file reorganization

BlackFlag does not rename, sort, or restructure the music folders behind your back.
